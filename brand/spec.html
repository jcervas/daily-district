Logo system

A wireframe globe, two hidden D's.

The Isoline mark is a limb, a polar axis, and meridians east of the axis — every line one a cartographer would already draw. Both hidden letters share the axis as their stem: the inner meridian closes a narrow D, the outer one a wider D around it — two D's for Daily District. One weight, one colour.

Construction

Limb, axis, meridians

Display cut · ring r44, stroke 6
Small cut · ring r45, stroke 8

100×100 units, centred on (50, 50). The limb is a plain stroked circle. The axis is a straight round-capped stroke through the centre, pole to pole. Each meridian is an elliptical arc sharing the limb's own radius as its ry, so it meets the limb exactly tangent at both poles — sharing a radius makes the arcs tangent by construction, not by nudging control points until they happen to line up (see History in the README). Dots mark the two poles, where the axis and every meridian converge; the dashed circle is the limb's own centreline.

Kept upright. The axis is polar, so rotating it away from vertical breaks the globe metaphor as well as the alignment — and a prior mark's diagonal variant was separately flagged as reading too close to a hate symbol and pulled immediately. Never rotate the mark, mirror the meridians to the west side of the axis, stretch the circle to an ellipse, or split the limb and meridians into two colours.

Optical sizes

One meridian drops out, not just thicker strokes

Below 24px the three concentric curves — the axis and two meridians — converge at both poles and start to merge, closing the counters. So the small cut drops the outer meridian (r28) entirely rather than just bolding the display cut: one D survives instead of two blurring together, and the remaining ring (r45) and strokes go bolder for the smallest real use on the site, the browser-tab favicon.

Horizontal: mark at full height, wordmark at native size 24 units clear of it, vertically centred (viewBox 0 0 384 100). Stacked: mark centred above the wordmark set to the full width, 20-unit gap. Both are currentColor — inline them and set color.

Colour

One colour, CMU Red on this site

The mark is a single colour that matches the "Daily District" wordmark beside it: CMU Red (#C41230), never a second colour inside the mark. On dark grounds red lifts to #FF3B57, because #C41230 goes muddy below about 20% ground luminance — a rendering correction, not a new brand colour. Navy (#182C4B) stays in the kit as the alternate plate.

RuleValueWhy
