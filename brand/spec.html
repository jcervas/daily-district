Logo system

A ring split into two D's.

The Split Ring is a circle bisected by a vertical S-spine. The spine sweeps the top of the ring to the right and the bottom to the left, so the circle reads as two interlocking D letterforms — two D's for Daily District — and, at a glance, as a single continuous stroke folding back on itself. One weight, one colour, all strokes with round caps and joins.

Construction

One ring, one S-spine

Display cut · ring r44, stroke 6
Small cut · ring r45, stroke 10

100×100 units, centred on (50, 50). The ring is a plain circle; the spine is two mirrored cubic Bézier curves that meet at the centre and land on the ring at top and bottom. Because the spine is 180°-rotationally symmetric about the centre, the two D regions are one shape and its turn. Dots mark the spine's five on-curve anchors; the dashed circle is the ring's centreline.

Kept upright. A prior mark's diagonal variant was flagged as reading too close to a hate symbol and pulled immediately; since then, anything set on a diagonal or with a radiating structure is off the table. Never rotate the mark to a diagonal, stretch the circle to an ellipse, square off the caps, redraw the spine, or split the ring and spine into two colours.

Optical sizes

The small cut is redrawn, not shrunk

The display cut carries the ring and spine in thin strokes, and thin strokes are the first thing lost to a raster. Below 24px the strokes silt up and the interior closes to a solid disc. The small cut fattens the ring (r45) and the spine (stroke 10) and pulls the spine's shoulders wider, so the two D's stay open at favicon sizes.

Colour

One colour, CMU Red on this site

The mark is a single colour that matches the "Daily District" wordmark beside it: CMU Red (#C41230). Red is also the in-product solved fill (mark-solved.svg), never a second colour inside the resting mark. On dark grounds red lifts to #FF3B57, because #C41230 goes muddy below about 20% ground luminance — a rendering correction, not a new brand colour. Navy (#182C4B) stays in the kit as the alternate plate.
